          About the model

          EVOPT Maps is a course project revived as a working demo. It shows its reasoning, so here is exactly what it assumes.

          How a trip is planned

          The roads of the chosen area come from OpenStreetMap. The planner searches for the fastest trip while tracking the battery. It charges only at real stations, only when needed, and only as much as it pays off in time.

          The search runs over a state made of a place and a battery level, so it can trade a longer charge now against a shorter one later. Several algorithms solve it, from Dijkstra to ARA*; the Algorithms page compares them on your trip.

          How energy is estimated

          For every road segment: rolling resistance plus air drag at the segment speed, divided by drivetrain efficiency, plus the power of heating or cooling over the time spent on it.

          energy = (Crr·m·g + ½·ρ·CdA·v²) · distance / efficiency + (0.5 kW + 0.1 kW per °C outside 18–24 °C) · time

          Cold and heat therefore cost range. Vehicle figures are approximate values from public data sheets.

          Where data comes from

          • Roads and charging stations: OpenStreetMap contributors.
          • Missing station power and extra stations: Open Charge Map, when a key is configured.
          • Map tiles: © OpenStreetMap contributors, shown in grey.

          What it does not know

          • Traffic, road works, hills and braking energy recovery.
          • Whether a charger is free or working right now.
          • Charging slows near full; the model charges at a constant power, so long stops are slightly optimistic.
          • Where OpenStreetMap lacks a station's power it assumes 22 kW.
